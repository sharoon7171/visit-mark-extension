VisitMark - Highlight visited links with custom colors — Privacy Policy

Last updated: March 31, 2026

In this policy, “we” and “us” mean the extension’s publisher, and “the extension” means this product. The extension changes how visited links look on pages you open. It runs on your device and uses Chrome’s extension APIs. This document explains what information is involved and what we do with it.

Information the extension processes

The extension may process the following categories of information, depending on your settings and the pages you visit.

  • Your preferences stored in Chrome sync storage (chrome.storage.sync), including highlight colors, global on/off state, per-website overrides, and related toggles.
  • Tab and navigation context that Chrome exposes to extensions, such as the URL of the active tab and when a page finishes loading or its main address changes, so the extension knows when to update highlighting.
  • Link addresses on web pages on http and https sites: the extension reads a[href] values from the page in order to match visited styling and, when you enable it, to compare against history.
  • Browsing history signals (optional) if you enable history-based visited detection: the extension uses Chrome’s History API (chrome.history.getVisits) to see whether URLs corresponding to links on the page appear in your browsing history. It also responds to history change events so highlights can refresh.

How we use that information

We use the information above only to operate the extension: applying and updating visited-link colors and the options you control in the extension popup. We do not use it for advertising, sale of data, or building profiles about you for unrelated purposes.

Where information is stored and who receives it

The extension does not send your browsing history, page content, or lists of links from websites to us or to servers we operate for VisitMark.

Processing happens in your browser through Chrome. If you use Chrome while signed in and sync is enabled, Google may synchronize extension sync storage according to your Chrome and Google account settings. Chrome’s History API and sync are provided by Google; how Google handles data from Chrome is described in Google’s own policies, not in this document.

Your choices

  • Turn off history-based detection in the extension popup if you do not want the extension to consult the History API.
  • Turn off highlighting globally or for specific sites using the popup.
  • Remove the extension or clear browsing and extension data using Chrome’s settings.

Changes to this policy

We may update this policy when the extension changes. The version on the page where this policy is published, together with the “Last updated” date at the top, is the one that applies.
